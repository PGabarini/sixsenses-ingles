Do we have a sixth sense?

Love, intuition, ghosts… and the senses science forgot to count

Pablo David Gabarini

What we learnt at school

Five senses. Only five?

Sight
Hearing
Smell
Taste
Touch
?

The list of five comes from Aristotle, around 350 BC.

Throughout history, people have put forward a “sixth sense”.

The Lady and the Unicorn, c. 1500 · Musée de Cluny, Paris

Candidate 1

Love

A 500-year-old idea: five tapestries for the five senses, and a mysterious sixth.

“To my only desire”, often read as love.

What science says

+ +

The brain combines sight, sound and even smell to shape attraction. No new sense needed.

Candidate 2

Intuition

The “gut feeling” that tells us what to do before we can explain why.

gut feeling
  1. 1

    Hidden emotional images led to faster, more confident decisionsUniversity of New South Wales

  2. 2

    Experienced traders’ gut feelings turned out to be real signals from their bodies

What science says

Intuition is real, but it isn’t magic: it’s the brain working too fast for us to notice.

Candidate 3

Extrasensory perception

Telepathy · premonitions · seeing ghosts

the “sixth sense” of the movies

In Celtic tradition, the gift was called “second sight”.

The Ganzfeld telepathy experiments looked promising…

…but other laboratories couldn’t repeat the results.

The verdict

  • Love
  • Intuition
  • ESP

None of them has enough evidence to be a separate sense.

So was Aristotle right after all?

Back to Aristotle

One organ, one sense

  • Eyes
  • Ears
  • Nose
  • Tongue
  • Skin

= five senses, and “no sixth sense”.

The problem

Some organs are hidden. The balance organ inside the ear was only understood in the early 1800s.

Source: Aristotle, De Anima (On the Soul), Book III

Forgotten sense 2

Balance

Also called the vestibular sense

  1. 1

    A tiny, fluid-filled organ in the inner ear

  2. 2

    It detects the movement of our head and the pull of gravity

  3. 3

    We only notice it when something goes wrong — like feeling seasick

Forgotten sense 3

Interoception

Feeling what’s happening inside our body

  • Heartbeat
  • Breathing
  • Hunger
  • Thirst
  1. 1

    People who are good at sensing their own heartbeat tend to be more intuitive

An interesting connection

Part of our “intuition” may actually come from this sense.

Nociception, a sense with its own receptors

    Not just a strong form of touch: a separate sense
